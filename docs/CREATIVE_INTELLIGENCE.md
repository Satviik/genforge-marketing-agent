# Creative Intelligence: Reel and Post Reverse-Engineering

GenForge should study content construction—not merely count views or copy trending topics. This layer turns high-performing references into a structured, reusable **creative blueprint** for producing original GenForge content.

## The two analysis tracks

### Reels / short-form video
1. **Objective:** reach, saves, shares, comments, product discovery, conversions, or community.
2. **First frame:** what is visible before a viewer hears anything; framing, motion, text, contrast, and product presence.
3. **Hook (0–3 seconds):** exact hook type and mechanism—curiosity gap, problem, transformation, unexpected visual, question, challenge, claim, or demonstration. Record observations, not assumptions.
4. **Beat map:** divide the video into timestamped beats. For each, note narration/text, visual, action, and purpose.
5. **Retention devices:** open loop, progress bar, pattern interrupt, escalating stakes, delayed reveal, rapid demonstration, cut on action, comparison, loop ending, etc.
6. **Proof:** how the creator makes the promise credible—demonstration, close-up detail, process, user reaction, comparison, testimonial, or evidence.
7. **Payoff:** reveal, finished product, answer, satisfying process, or transformation.
8. **CTA:** requested action, timing, wording, and fit with the objective.
9. **Editing and audio:** cut frequency, camera movement, transitions, captions, music, sound effects, and silence. Describe patterns rather than simply “fast editing.”
10. **Replication boundary:** the mechanism GenForge can adapt, and elements that must remain original (product, footage, script, voice, branding, design).

### Static posts / carousels
1. **Purpose:** save-worthy tutorial, shareable opinion, product showcase, comparison, reference guide, or conversion.
2. **Cover:** headline length, promise, image, contrast, and hierarchy.
3. **Slide-by-slide structure:** each slide's job—hook, context, problem, steps, evidence, example, conclusion, CTA.
4. **Information architecture:** text density, visual vs. written information, and how the viewer progresses.
5. **Visual hierarchy:** focal object, type size, contrast, spacing, color, repeated layout, and consistency.
6. **Payoff and CTA:** utility of the final slide and next action.
7. **Caption:** opening line, expansion, useful detail, conversation prompt, CTA, and optional keywords/hashtags.

## Creative DNA record
Capture content format, objective, first frame and hook text, hook type/mechanism, story structure, timestamped beat map, retention devices, proof, payoff, CTA, caption/carousel structure, visual hierarchy, visual patterns, editing/audio, psychological triggers, comment prompt, adaptable principles, replication notes, analysis notes, and confidence.

## Reference study protocol
- Prefer references with observable metrics, but metrics do not reveal why content worked.
- Inspect the entire Reel or all carousel slides—not only the cover or viral snippet.
- Make timestamps approximate and explicitly mark uncertain observations.
- Compare references within a similar niche and format where possible.
- Separate **observed facts**, **interpretations**, and **hypotheses**.
- Do not infer causality from one example.
- Never copy exact scripts, shots, distinctive artwork, or creator branding. Learn the pattern and rebuild with original GenForge products and footage.

## From reference to production brief
For each studied reference, create a brief containing:
- **Pattern to borrow:** underlying structure, not copied content.
- **GenForge adaptation:** product and original angle.
- **Beat-by-beat script:** narration, on-screen text, shot, and timing.
- **Shot list:** camera angle, framing, movement, lighting, props, and product action.
- **Edit plan:** pacing, cuts, transitions, captions, sound cues.
- **Caption + CTA:** tailored to the objective.
- **Quality checklist:** hook clarity, visual proof, accuracy, brand, originality, and mobile legibility.

## Pattern library and capability boundary
Use `radar creative-patterns` to aggregate recurring observed structures. Frequency counts describe the reference set only—not causality or a virality guarantee. The current schema accepts analyst-supplied notes (or future media-analysis adapters); GenForge does not yet automatically inspect a Reel's timeline from its URL.
